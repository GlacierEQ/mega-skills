# Chemistry & Materials Engineering Layer

## Thermodynamics First

- Equilibrium (ΔG, phase stability, solubility, equilibrium constants) before kinetics.
- Identify the driving force (chemical potential, activity, concentration, partial pressure).
- Confirm the desired state is thermodynamically allowed under operating conditions.

## Kinetics & Rates

- Rate-limiting step identification usually outweighs a full mechanism.
- Activation energy, pre-factor, and temperature sensitivity (Arrhenius).
- Catalysts, inhibitors, and surface effects often dominate industrial reality.
- Mass-transport limitations frequently mask intrinsic kinetics at scale.

## Transport Phenomena Coupling

- Reaction + diffusion (Thiele modulus, effectiveness factor).
- Heat release/absorption coupled to temperature-dependent rates.
- Mixing, residence-time distribution, and scale-up implications.

## Materials Selection Heuristics

- Strength, stiffness, toughness, density, cost, corrosion, temperature limits, processability.
- Think in Ashby terms: specific strength, specific stiffness, etc.
- Processing route often constrains final microstructure more than nominal composition.

## Practical Checks

- Chemistry compatible with containment materials and seals?
- Side reactions, runaway risk, or toxic intermediates?
- Atom economy and waste stream?
- Continuous process feasible, or is batch forced by the physics/chemistry?
